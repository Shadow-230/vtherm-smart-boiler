# The 0.2.1 review compared with the 0.2 audit and the 0.2.1 change log — 2026-09-26

This compares three documents:

- the **audit of 0.2**: `docs/review-2026-09-24.md` (code at `16f0448`), with its full data in the git-ignored
  `research/2026-09-24-code-review/`;
- the **change log of 0.2.1**: `session-summary-2026-09-26.md` (the session summary in the repository root, in
  Polish, untracked) — 89 commits from L1 (`5c553f1`) to the close of R6 (`0f101ee`), read together with
  `docs/plan-0.2.1.md` and the git history `16f0448..0f101ee`;
- the **review of 0.2.1**: `docs/review-2026-09-26.md` (code at `0f101ee`).

It answers three questions: what 0.2.1 did with each finding of the audit, and whether the change log says so
truly; where each of the 190 problems of the new review comes from; and what the pattern means for K4. Read-only:
nothing in the code, the specification or the plans was changed.

**Method.** Nine tasks traced every item through the change log, the plan, the commits (`git log --grep`, `git
blame`, `git show 16f0448:`) and the code at `0f101ee`: the audit's 111 code findings, 32 specification findings,
10 open questions and top 5; the 52 items of the change log's sections 3b–3e; the new review's 190 problems. Every
claim of a regression, an incomplete fix, a problem introduced by 0.2.1, an unrecorded open problem or an
inaccurate change-log entry — 204 claims — went to a second agent told to refute it: 135 were confirmed, 68
corrected (most often toward a milder class, e.g. "never fully fixed" instead of "recurred"), 1 left undecided.
The numbers below use the corrected classes.

## 1. Summary

1. **0.2.1 fixed the substance of the audit.** Of the 111 code findings, 64 are fixed, 37 partly, 7 became moot
   through the decisions of 2026-09-25 (anti-cycling, budgets and the daily cap removed), and 3 fixes introduced a
   new problem (regressions). Of the 19 critical and high findings, 11 are fixed, 6 partly and 2 moot; none
   regressed. The ordered OTGW hand-back of P02 is fixed, but its crash path with CH=0 stays (the new review's
   S-01); P01 is fixed.
2. **The fixes are often narrower than the findings.** 37 code and 18 specification findings are only partly fixed,
   yet the plan's index and the change log count them as done. The residue is visible in the new review: 71 of
   its 190 problems are the unfinished part of an audit or R6 item.
3. **Three fixes introduced regressions:** P30 (U1 put the version detection before the store is read → the new
   review's P-04), P52 / question 9 (the fail-closed VT central-boiler check makes a disabled VT central entry
   block control for ever → P-20), P69 (the narrower diagnostics redaction stopped masking absent entities →
   P-30). Question 7 was answered by allowing a stand-alone power or opening criterion, which opened P-14 and S-04.
4. **The change log is right in outline and generous in detail.** After verification no entry is plainly false,
   but 46 of the 206 entries that make a claim overstate it. The log does not mention any problem its own fixes
   introduced (for example P-21 from the C1 fix, P-04 from U1, P-30 from U12).
5. **The new review mostly finds what the audit and the log did not.** Its 190 problems: 71 incomplete fixes, 59
   present already at `16f0448` and missed by the audit, 36 introduced by 0.2.1, 10 following from the decisions of
   2026-09-25, 14 already known and recorded. The change log names 23 of them, fully or in part.
6. **None of the five high problems was introduced by 0.2.1.** P-02 (control switch unavailable), P-03 (the same
   switch in two fields) and S-02 (hard minimum not tied to the boiler) existed at `16f0448` and were missed;
   P-01 (corrupt store) and S-01 (CH=0 after a crash) are incomplete fixes — for S-01 the audit's own data held the
   question (finding "critic-14"), but it never reached the report's tables, the plan or the log.
7. **The trend is clearly better.** Critical 2 → 0, high 17 → 5 (code 17 → 3), tests 575 → 911, coverage
   95 % → 96.75 %, mypy strict and formatting clean. The raw counts of medium and low problems grew because the new
   review graded the specification in the same table and at a finer grain; section 6 shows most of them are not new.

### 3.3 Regressions

- **P30 → P-04 (medium).** P30 was a blocking package-metadata read in the event loop. U1 (`2c94d19`) moved the
  read into the executor, but placed `link.async_detect()` in `async_start` before `_async_load_store`; if it
  raises, `async_stop` writes the default state over the real store (the controlling marker, latches, the
  monitoring start). The trigger is unlikely; the effect is the same as P-01.
- **P52 and question 9 → P-20 (medium).** P52 was control starting while VT's central boiler could still be
  configured. N2 (`2dc710f`) made the check fail closed, and `0a90196` returns "unknown" whenever the owner entry
  is not loaded. A VT central entry the user disabled, or one stuck in SETUP_ERROR, now blocks control for ever and
  hands back (stand-alone: no heating); the R6 fix H1 covered only the "unavailable" placeholder.
- **P69 → P-30 (medium).** P69 asked diagnostics to redact only personal data. U12 (`f12add1`) narrowed the
  redaction to known entities and entities named in the options, but `_named_entities` handles `dict` while the
  options are a `MappingProxyType`, so entities that are absent are no longer masked.
- **Question 7 → P-14, S-04 (medium).** N3 allowed a power or opening criterion to stand alone, as in VT. With VT
  publishing a device power of 0 when none is configured, such a criterion never calls for heat; with zero zones and
  a count threshold of 0, control heats all year.

### 3.4 Not fixed, or open

- **S06 (not fixed).** Stand-alone gateway after a hand-back with a latch: the audit proposed a fallback setpoint or
  an expiring latch; the decision of 2026-09-25 chose "alarm and stop" instead. The consequences are in the new
  review's P-08, S-10 and S-57.
- **Question 1 (open, known).** Whether "off" as a low setpoint stops the CH pump — the L3 facts say mostly not; the
  decision is L4 🔒.
- **S-01 (open, unrecorded).** The CH=0-after-a-crash risk was in the audit's data (critic-14) and is in neither the
  plan nor "Open after R6".

## 7. Patterns and what to do with them

1. **An item is closed when its tested scenario passes, not when the whole finding is gone.** 37 code and 18
   specification findings are partly fixed yet marked done. Close an audit item only against every scenario in
   `research/2026-09-24-code-review/findings.md`, and move any remainder into "Open after R6" by name.
2. **New mechanisms bring new edge cases** (the drop rule, the CH echo, fail-closed detection, "pressure 0",
   narrowed redaction, the daily model). Give each new mechanism a negative test for an unknown, missing or `None`
   input before it is marked done.
3. **The decisions of 2026-09-25 moved risk into the specification** — heating with no zone known in every season,
   frost protection watching zones whose valves VT closes, no activation delay, a verdict that counts what control
   does not address. These belong on the K4 agenda with their physical effect stated.
4. **The audit missed three high problems**, all in areas it rated as fine or did not reach: entity availability,
   validation across form fields, the boiler's minimum against the curve. A later review should ask those kinds of
   questions explicitly.
5. **Read the change log with this comparison at K4.** It is a reliable map of what was done; about one claim in
   four and a half overstates its reach, and it names only 23 of the 190 problems found afterwards.

Unrecorded today and worth adding to "Open after R6": S-01 (CH=0 after a crash, the audit's critic-14), P-20 (a
disabled VT central entry), P-01 and P-04 (lost control state), P-21 (the `opentherm_gw` write / hand-back
asymmetry).
